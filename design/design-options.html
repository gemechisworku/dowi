A · Money report

Structure

  • Top bar: greeting + date on the left, bell with badge and settings avatar on the right. Detail screens swap to back-arrow + title.
  • Bottom nav: floating rounded bar, 4 tabs, active tab is a filled blue pill.
  • Primary action: square-ish FAB above the nav, contextual per tab.

Visual language

  • 22 px radii, soft shadows, white cards on a light-gray canvas.
  • A single blue gradient hero carries the headline number.
  • Icons in tinted squircle chips; colour used generously.

Best if you want

  • The warmest, most approachable feel; closest to what people expect from a modern Android app.
  • Quick-action tiles right on Home.

Trade-off

  • Cards and padding cost vertical space — fewer rows visible per screen.

B · Money report

Structure

  • Top bar: app/screen title + date context, bell with count and a direct settings gear.
  • Second row: a persistent scrollable tab strip for period/section switching — the period control lives in the chrome, not in the content.
  • Bottom nav: full-width, edge-to-edge, active tab marked by a top rule + blue label.
  • Primary action: labelled "＋ Add" pill, bottom-right.

Visual language

  • 12 px radii, 1 px borders instead of shadows, tight 10 px rows.
  • Uppercase micro-labels, tabular numerals, coloured status dots.
  • Colour reserved for meaning (green/red/amber), blue for interaction only.

Best if you want

  • To see the most at a glance — roughly 60 % more rows per screen than A.
  • Reports to feel like a real finance tool.

Trade-off

  • Denser tap targets; less playful. Needs care to stay accessible.

🏠📊 Money📝✅
C · Money report

Structure

  • Top bar: minimal — a small date eyebrow plus bell and gear. The screen title is a large headline in the content instead.
  • Bottom nav: a floating dark pill; only the active tab shows its label, the rest are icons.
  • Sections are separated by whitespace and hairlines — no card containers at all.

Visual language

  • Display typography (Plus Jakarta Sans) for headlines and key numbers.
  • Blue used sparingly as a true accent; green/red only on money and status.
  • Generous 22 px side margins, calm and uncluttered.

Best if you want

  • Something that feels distinctive rather than templated.
  • Writing (Notes) and reflection (weekly review) to feel pleasant.

Trade-off

  • The least information per screen; long lists need more scrolling. The icon-only nav is slightly less obvious at first.

How to choose

All three share the same tokens, the same four-tab information architecture and the same component contracts — so the choice changes the look and the density, not the plan or the schedule. Mixing is allowed: for example A for Home and Notes with B's density for Money lists and reports.
